BLK II - UNIT 5: METOC Mission Products
d. Terminal Aerodrome Forecast (TAF)
Introduction to the TAF
You already learned how to decode TAFs and what reference material to utilize. You will now
use your forecasting abilities and your knowledge on decoding TAFs to encode your forecast
data into TAF format.
You will start by completing the forecast reasoning worksheet and Local Area Forecast section
of the MEF. Then, using the TAF Specification Criteria below, determine where the appropriate
BECMG and TEMPO lines should be and format a TAF based on your forecast elements.
Reference Manuals & Required Data
The reference material for this objective will be the same as the unit in Block I where you
learned to decode the TAF. The 15-124 will be your source to encode all required elements for
your TAF.
All branches will forecast and encode a 30-hour TAF for their location.
The main challenge of encoding a TAF is ensuring that it is horizontally consistent with all other
products you create. The next challenge is to determine where the thresholds exist in your
forecast that require a new line in your TAF. You must also determine if certain forecast
elements are expected to occur for more than half of the hour to decide if you need a new
BECMG line or if a TEMPO line would suffice.
Use the table below to determine where your breaks need to be, as well as ensuring your TAF
remains “in category”. If one of the following thresholds are crossed and your TAF does not
reflect the appropriate data, it is considered “out of category” and requires amending.
